Understanding Miller Indices (hkl) & Crystal Planes

Miller Indices are a universal notation system in materials science used to define crystallographic planes in crystal lattices, based on the reciprocals of their fractional intercepts along the x, y, and z axes.

The 4-Step Process

  1. Step 1:

    Find the Intercepts on the x, y, and z axes in terms of lattice constants a, b, c.

  2. Step 2:

    Take the Reciprocals of these intercept values.

  3. Step 3:

    Clear Fractions by finding the least common denominator (LCD) and multiplying.

  4. Step 4:

    Write in Parentheses as (h k l) without commas.

Key Rules & Edge Cases

∞

Infinity & Zero: If a plane is parallel to an axis, its intercept is infinity (∞), meaning its Miller index is 0.

0

Passing Through the Origin: If a plane passes directly through the origin (0,0,0), its intercepts are zero. You must translate/shift the origin to a parallel position (by an integer multiple of unit cell lengths) to avoid zero-intercept ambiguity.

$\bar{1}$

Negative Intercepts: Indicated by an overbar (e.g., $\bar{1}$). To visualize, shift the origin of your coordinate system to a different corner of the unit cell so the negative axis direction falls cleanly inside your active box.

CRITICAL RULE: Never multiply or divide by -1 just to clear a bar (e.g., do not convert $(\bar{1}\ \bar{1}\ \bar{1})$ into $(1\ 1\ 1)$). Changing signs alters the orientation and implies false symmetry.

Practical Example

Intercepts:
2, 3, 2
Reciprocals:
1/2, 1/3, 1/2
Multiply by LCD (6):
3, 2, 3

Final Miller Indices:

(3 2 3)
